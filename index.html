Historical Analysis & Interpretation

    What if your neighbor next door was an anarchist that was preaching death to America every morning? Or they were saluting a red soviet flag? That was the red scare in a nutshell because everybody was terrified of immigrants who embraced communism or anarchism. Mainly Russian, eastern and southern Europe immigrants were feared the most. They were suspected the most for being radical. People were dying, and being jailed for expressing their freedom of speech. Many states and people were being sent bombs in the mail. The government was conducting raids all across America. Thousands of people who were even suspected of being radical were deported or jailed. Radicals tried to have a work union and attack the IWW. 
    A sailor had shot and killed a man, because he wouldn’t stand up for the star spangled banner; people clapped and cheered. A man was brought to court after killing an immigrant who had yelled “To hell with the United States”. The judge had taken two minutes to say he was not guilty. The congress and state legislatures also joined in the fight against communism. The House denied Victor Berger, because he was charged with sedition. He wouldn’t be able to run until 1922 when the charge was dropped. Immigrants from Russia, Eastern and Southern Europe were denied entry into America due to the fear of radicalism. Thousands of aliens were also deported because they were suspected of being radical. In modern times immigrants wouldn't be barred entry into the United States because of the country they're coming from. There is also proof in modern times that aliens would be deported en masse if suspected of something.
    There were also mass bombings happening. In May, 1919, multiple bombs were mailed to capitalist, and some government officials. Then a month later eight American cities were bombed. One of the bombs near Wall street in Manhattan’s financial district had killed 30 people, and injured hundreds. It was left in a horse drawn carriage. The government had put up an $80,000 reward for information on the suspect who drove the carriage. It was suspected that a radical European immigrant was the suspect. Nobody was ever charged for this crime. After these bombings the government had responded by conducting raids all across America; arresting thousands.  The largest raid for this time-period was on April 29, 1920. Over four thousand people that were suspected of being a radical were arrested nationwide.
